No AI Slop is Peter Yang's editing skill for text that sounds machine-written. It goes after the recognisable patterns: "It's not X. It's Y." contrasts, throat-clearing openers, faux-insight setups, dramatic one-line fragments, importance puffery, weasel attributions and fake-profound endings. It also nudges the basics, like leading with the point, using the active voice and preferring concrete detail.

Unlike a generic "make this better" prompt, it's built to leave your vocabulary, rhythm and humour alone, and it lists what it changed so you can check its work. It can also flag patterns without rewriting anything, which helps when you want to judge a draft rather than hand it over.

**When to use it:** polishing posts, newsletters, docs or emails you drafted with AI help, reviewing someone else's text, or setting a house standard for agent-written copy.

**Caveats:** it fixes patterns, not ideas; a thin argument stays thin after editing. It's one focused skill, so pair it with your own style guide if you have one.
